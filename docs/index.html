Orders and campaign pacing

Use RECON / RAID / TERROR in the target popup. RAID means Rural Harvest on land and City Abduction in a city. Research names and mission requirements still distinguish these roles. Ocean and unsupported terrain allow RECON only. TERROR means the early Rural Terror Raid on land; City Terror still needs a Dreadnought or Warship and its existing crew/payload authorization.

Harvest supplies Resource Caches for paid Reserve Processing. An unopposed Scout targets 2 caches, a Surveyor 3, a Harvester 6 and a Warship 8. A battle uses its measured tactical objective and recovery receipt instead; losses or retreat can reduce the return. Only a specialist full Harvest or a completed tactical objective grants full Harvest progression. Two caches can become 24 Elerium, but processing also consumes $110,000, 20 Construction Material, 4 Allocation and 640 Fabricator-hours. They are processing inputs, not instant free fuel. The collecting UFO must actually return; cargo goes to its own base, once.

Harvest and Abduction remain on site for 12 hours after actual landing. RECON stays 2 hours, Rural Terror 12 hours, City Terror 24 hours. Civilian witnesses can report ordinary raids outside radar. Rural Terror reports build over time. A discovered operation does not guarantee battle: a living, staffed X-COM base must send a real available transport with fuel and enough time to reach it. If you want early ground action, raid or terrorize within reach of known or suspected X-COM activity. Distant supply sorties are useful but may stay quiet. City Terror is public and invokes the existing mobilization/replacement rules, with actual travel.

Small and Large X-COM radar now use the original-scale 1695 and 2577 nautical-mile coverage. Hyperwave coverage is at least 2759. Conventional contact is a chance each hour, with hull visibility, local alertness and enemy research modifiers; it is not a guaranteed counter reaching 100. Arrival and the hourly clock cannot roll twice in the same hour, including after a save/reload. Existing built radars adopt the corrected coverage on load. These are an hourly adaptation of original radar ideas, not an exact simulation of every original half-hour mechanic.

An airborne UFO can be intercepted. A landed UFO receives a ground response instead of a new dogfight; an interceptor that arrives after landing cannot shoot the landed UFO down. Wider coverage makes defended approaches matter. Preserve crew, avoid repeating costly losses, upgrade through research and inspect reports rather than scheduling every craft blindly. On Veteran and above, repeated early city Abductions by a Medium Scout near X-COM interceptor coverage can exhaust the reserve before a sturdier hull arrives. Vary targets and preserve the Scout.

Alien players can build multiple bases through BUILD NEW BASE, up to the native eight-base limit. Founding, facilities, staff, craft and upkeep still cost money. Establish a useful forward listening/operating site when its coverage or shorter flight times justify the expense; filling eight slots is optional. X-COM also pays for replacement aircraft, staff and bases. It expands progressively beyond three when its reserve and construction requirements permit; damaging bases or interrupting operations can slow that growth. There is no infinite-money switch or fixed universal expansion date.

Action depends on where you operate and how visible the mission is. Raids near X-COM activity create more chances for ground contact than distant surveys. Research, production, tactical objectives, and monthly standing all matter; there is no single launch count that wins the war.

Install and protect your saves

You need your own copy of X-COM: UFO Defense. Alien Conquest already includes the engine and mod.

  1. Extract the ZIP to a folder you can write to. It creates an AlienConquest folder
  2. Find the folder in your UFO Defense installation that contains GEODATA
  3. Copy GEODATA, GEOGRAPH, MAPS, ROUTES, SOUND, TERRAIN, UFOGRAPH, UFOINTRO, and UNITS directly into AlienConquest/UFO
  4. Run AlienConquest/openxcom.exe, choose New Game, and place your first ocean base

For example, the correct path is AlienConquest/UFO/GEODATA, not AlienConquest/UFO/XCOM/GEODATA. You do not need a second OpenXcom installation.

When updating, keep your old installation until you have tried the new one. Extract the release to a fresh folder, copy your own original-game data, and then copy the saves you want to continue from the old user/alien-conquest folder.

Make named saves at useful decision points: campaign opening, before a major purchase, before launch, after a difficult tactical recovery, and before accelerating across a month boundary. Rotate several slots. A launch save is valuable because it preserves the actual stores, assigned crew and fuel state; a save made only after entering battle cannot correct a forgotten stun launcher. Save before experimenting with an unfamiliar interface or mission family.

For a first campaign, use the lowest difficulty and ordinary saving. Learn the reversed objectives before choosing a harsher setting. Difficulty affects enemy pressure and combat, but paid enemy infrastructure can produce different growth paths. Do not assume that every higher difficulty reaches every strategic milestone on an identical or strictly earlier calendar date.

What you manage

The Geoscape is both a map and a schedule. Craft travel, operational exposure, enemy responses, fabrication, construction, recovery and monthly assessments all consume time. Keep the clock slow while making unfamiliar decisions. Return to the smallest time step after a report or new contact, inspect what changed, then accelerate only when the next few hours have a clear purpose.

The base is a shared pool of people, space and supplies. Biological Crew assigns living aliens to UFOs. Tanks assigns mechanical Cyberdiscs and Sectopods. Equipment stocks portable items on a craft and arranges eligible operators for battle. Research assigns Analysts. Manufacture assigns Fabricators. Purchase supplies authorized reinforcements and hulls; it is not a general human supermarket.

Distinguish three resources that can all sound like funding. Cash pays build costs, purchases and recipe costs. Authority is your standing with the Cydonian High Order and drives monetary support. Cydonian Allocation is a physical store item consumed by many fabrication recipes. A healthy bank balance does not substitute for an Allocation item. A good Authority number does not prove that the local workshop has the samples needed for a grant recipe.

Also distinguish the campaign's accumulated evidence from cargo in storage. Earth Intelligence Reports and similar proof items can support research and recipes, while the strategic ledger records the history of useful operations. Spending a physical report in a recipe does not automatically erase every past reconnaissance success. Conversely, possessing a fabricated report is not a promise that every operation-history threshold has been satisfied. Use the monthly strategic report for overall progress and the local base's stores for project availability.

The opening cell has eighteen biological Sectoids: fourteen Soldiers and one each of Navigator, Engineer, Medic and Leader. It also has ten Analysts and ten Fabricators. Its three hangars hold a ready Scout and a ready Probe, leaving one craft berth. The habitat and reproduction facilities together provide sixty personnel places; troop reserves and incoming staff share that space. The Research Chamber supports fifty Analysts and the Fabrication Chamber provides fifty workshop places. Project space and assigned workers both matter in manufacturing.

The starting supplies are generous enough to learn, but finite. The base reserve contains 800 Elerium, 120 Alien Alloys, 100 Alien Food, 80 Construction Material, 30 Genetic Material and 60 Cydonian Allocation. The Scout and Probe also carry their own equipment. Treat these as an opening runway: they let you establish a useful operation loop before you need to replace everything through fabrication and recovery.

Your first play session

1. Start a new Alien Conquest campaign, choose difficulty, place the initial base underwater on a valid ocean position, and give it a recognizable name. A coastal ocean position near population centers and reachable open country reduces awkward first trips. Land placement is rejected for the alien base. This placement rule does not make the base invisible or guarantee a particular enemy starting location.

2. Pause to inspect Bases. Open the troop list and identify the existing specialist castes. Keep at least one recognizable Soldier assigned to the Probe and a small useful section available to the Scout. You do not need to fill the Scout merely to inspect it. Decide whether to use three operators for a light Scout flight or six for a first Harvest. A full nine-person Scout gives more tactical options but concentrates more of your opening force in one possible interception.

3. Open the Scout's Crew screen. Left-click a unit row to load or unload it; right-click opens unit information. The list identifies species and current craft. Move people deliberately instead of relying entirely on the order popup's automatic assignment. Keep enough healthy reserve troops at the base to crew the Probe and replace a wound or casualty.

4. Open Equipment for the Scout. Its initial craft stock includes six Plasma Pistols, twelve pistol clips, two Plasma Rifles, four rifle clips, six Alien Grenades, a Mind Probe, a Small Launcher, four Stun Bombs, four Smoke Grenades and four Electro-Flares. Check that each intended shooter has a compatible weapon and ammunition. Give the stun specialist the Small Launcher and spare Stun Bombs. Assign a few grenades and smoke, not every heavy object to the same frail Sectoid. Keep the capture kit aboard even when scouting: an unexpected battle can supply your first live human.

5. Inspect the Probe. It carries one Plasma Pistol and clip, one smoke grenade and one flare. It has one biological slot and no craft weapon slot. Use it to Scout, test a route or maintain reconnaissance while the Scout returns. It is not an early one-person abduction craft.

6. Open Research, select New Project, and assign Analysts to First Wave Operations. Concentrating the opening ten Analysts on one short prerequisite is an easy way to see progress. Scout Flight is another immediate priority. Human, Earth Atmosphere and X-COM Structure are also available from the start. You can change the exact opening order; the key is to complete connected prerequisites while the craft gathers the missing proof items.

7. Open Manufacture. At the fresh start, Construction Material is the only authored recipe available without research. You already have eighty units, so manufacturing it immediately is optional. Do not spend your initial Allocation merely because a project is visible. Once First Wave Operations completes, a Field Plasma Cache is a useful replacement source. Once Scout Flight completes, inspect Scout Rearmament Cache and Elerium Reserve Processing.

8. Return to the map and click a nearby city. Select RECON explicitly. The popup starts at SELECT ORDER; opening it does not choose an operation for you. Use CRAFT > until the intended Probe or Scout is shown, then LAUNCH. If you only want to inspect the city, Cancel. A successful launch closes the popup and puts the UFO on a real visible flight.

9. Advance time slowly enough to notice contacts and reports. Read an operation result before launching another task. Concealed operations can complete without a tactical battle. Detected operations can lead to interception and a landed response. You cannot require every Scout mission to produce a firefight, and you cannot assume every apparently quiet landing will stay quiet.

10. When the first research completes, choose the next prerequisite. First Wave Operations plus Scout Flight opens Scout Recon. Human plus X-COM Structure opens Abduction Protocol. First Wave Operations plus Earth Atmosphere opens Resource Raids, followed by Resource Survey when a Resource Cache is present. Scout Recon plus X-COM Structure and an Earth Intelligence Report opens X-COM Response.

11. Use the Scout for an open-land RAID (Harvest) to pursue resources, or a city RAID (Abduction) to pursue samples and captives. These buttons describe different objectives. Carry the correct kit, assign enough people, and inspect fuel before launching. Bring survivors and cargo home; research samples must actually be available at the base that starts the project.

12. After each return, check Craft status, wounds, clips, stun ammunition, stores and unfinished research. Save. Your successful first session ends with a functioning operation loop and identified next prerequisites, not with a particular calendar date or a promise that all first battles were easy.

Preparing your first City Terror

Terror Deployment is available immediately. Assign the starting ten Analysts: native research variation takes three to nine daily research ticks. Purchase a Dreadnought for $2,600,000 and Cyberdiscs for $450,000 each. The craft takes exactly fourteen days to arrive; the platforms take one day. One craft with two platforms costs $3,500,000, leaving $2,500,000 before other purchases, upkeep or operational income.

A delivered Dreadnought still needs fuel, equipment, and biological crew. Set aside plasma weapons and clips while it is on order. One Cyberdisc provides the City Terror payload but does not count as crew. You may buy a second as a reserve, though one is enough for the payload. Your actual launch date depends on research time, delivery, fuel, and other purchases.

City Terror is public. X-COM must use a real surviving base, troops, fuel and transport to respond. The battle has the existing civilian objective and native combat/debrief; an early authorization does not award automatic panic, victory or recovered cargo. Alien losses and abandoned equipment remain dangerous. Quiet raids still support resources and specimens. A second base remains a paid alternative use of your budget.

Terror Doctrine remains advanced strategic research toward Earth Capitulation. It is not required to deploy the newly authorized craft. Warship, Chryssalid, Sectopod and other advanced authorizations remain unchanged. Existing saves can study the new Terror Deployment topic; no craft, platform, research or money is granted on load.

Do not demolish useful opening facilities simply to create a beautiful diagram. A functioning connection to the Command Nexus matters more than symmetrical placement. The hangar uses a two-by-two footprint and houses one UFO. Leave usable connected space for expansions and avoid buying a fourth craft while only three completed hangars exist. Develop a clear role: forward craft support, research, production, or reserve staging. Do not split every specialist and every clip equally just because two bases exist. A forward Scout without the ammunition it uses is a stranded investment. Concealment Matrix and Hyper-Wave Nexus are later tools with exact research gates; build them for the strategic problem they solve.

Cash, Authority, fuel, and ammunition

Alien monetary support equals Authority, clamped to zero through 220, multiplied by 13,000. At Authority 100 that is 1,300,000 in monthly income; at 150 it is 1,950,000. Maintenance is then charged through the usual financial flow. A new campaign also receives a one-time 2,000,000-credit expedition reserve. This comes from the High Order, not national funding agreements. Raising Authority helps both your strategic position and your recurring budget, while poor assessments make recovery harder.

Keep a cash reserve for monthly upkeep and one meaningful emergency: replacement crew, a replacement Scout, or a supply run. A bank balance that can pay for a Warship today may still leave you unable to operate it next month. Check staff costs and facility maintenance on the financial screens before committing to a large expansion. Alien troop rules have zero salary, but staff and buildings are still part of the base economy.

UFO fuel uses Elerium. The hull table lists fuel capacity and refueling rate; the fuel gauge is not a direct count of identical Elerium store units. Leave Elerium for flight support as well as recipes. Fabricating Heavy Plasma clips and late craft weapons can consume the same strategic resource you need to keep reconnaissance running. If production would empty the reserve, reduce the queue quantity rather than assuming a harvest will arrive in time.

Elerium Reserve Processing converts a Resource Cache, ten Construction Material and two Allocation into twelve Elerium, at its stated cash and labor cost. It is a useful replenishment route, not free energy. Supply Grant converts physical proof items into Allocation. Final Logistics Resupply is a late combined packet. Use the complete recipe table to plan the whole chain before consuming your final copy of an input needed for the next research project.

Weapon clips are individual objects. A gun in the craft list is not a loaded gun in a unit's hand. Plasma Pistol clips, Plasma Rifle clips and Heavy Plasma clips are distinct. Load the right one and carry a spare where appropriate. Do not take eight rifles and eight pistol clips and expect the equipment screen to solve compatibility. After battle, check losses and surviving clips; the same craft equipment template may request more than the base now possesses.

Smoke and flares deserve a place in the routine queue once you have the required fabrication authorization. A cheap support item can prevent an expensive casualty. Stun Bombs are especially easy to underestimate because an unsuccessful attempt still consumes ammunition. A capture plan should have more than one shot and a carrier who can reach the unconscious body. Retain some ordinary lethal weapons to protect that carrier.

The replicated human laser family offers carried weapons without conventional magazines. That reduces one field supply burden, but obtaining the sample and completing the analysis still costs campaign effort. Human cannons and missiles are craft armaments; their ammunition belongs to the craft system. Do not confuse Cannon Rounds with a soldier's Heavy Cannon shell. Names can sound similar while storage and use differ.

The biological Crew list includes all living species, including Reapers, Chryssalids, Silacoids and Celatids. Their innate attacks do not make them mechanical. Cyberdiscs and Sectopods use Tanks and do not satisfy biological operator requirements. They take large-unit space and have fixed native armaments. Manual assignment gives you control over who risks the flight and who stays behind. Before LAUNCH, inspect the displayed craft, its readiness and fuel, and the crew already aboard. An idle icon alone does not prove the craft is READY; repairing, rearming and refueling are different states.

The crew descriptions help you plan a balanced team. For launch, the practical checks are enough healthy biological operators, a craft suited to the operation, fuel, and any required terror payload. A Navigator is useful, but every launch does not have a separate Navigator checkbox.

Targets, landings, and X-COM response

City clicks expose Scout, Abduct and city Terrorize. Open ground exposes Scout, Harvest and rural Terrorize. Ocean or unsupported terrain falls back to Scout only. City Harvest and open-ground Abduct are deliberately excluded. A city marker is a permanent operation target, not evidence that a human terror emergency is already in progress. Ordinary marker visibility follows the game's normal globe presentation and zoom behavior.

Probe is reconnaissance only. Scout and Surveyor provide the early working landings. Harvester specializes in rural biological and material extraction; Abductor specializes in humans from population centers. Dreadnought delivers public city terror, and Warship provides late command pressure and base assault. Carrier is a later logistical hull; buying it does not add a fifth opening map button or allow every operation everywhere.

Research names and fabrication projects can mention infiltration, installations, resource raids and simulations. These belong to the strategic support tree. The v1.0 main target popup deliberately offers the four clear operation families above. Completing a project called Infiltration does not promise that a new INFILTRATE button appears on every city. A support cache is equipment or paperwork, not a teleporting mission launcher.

A normal operation has travel, time on site and return behavior. Scout, Harvest and Abduction can remain concealed and finish without a battle. City Terror is public: X-COM dispatches a real available Skyranger from a surviving base with troops. City terror allows emergency lift with travel capped at eight hours and can wait up to twenty-four hours for a busy transport to mobilize. Rural Terror is different. It must be discovered through radar contact or accumulating field reports. A brief remote raid can finish unseen. If detected, a staffed base must have a fueled transport that can reach the raid before its original on-site deadline. Rural raids continue their work and leave on schedule; they do not wait for distant or unavailable troops. Responders leave their actual home base, spend fuel, and take time to arrive. Only arrival produces the battlemap. Destroyed bases, lost transports, exhausted fuel or absent troops cannot supply a response. No operation spends a terror response transport before actual landing.

Scouting is a survey, not a substitute for the rest of the invasion. A fresh target earns full standing and progress credit. Repeated unopposed surveys close to the same location within a month earn less, then only monitoring credit. After a quiet month, the area can again be worth surveying. A battle victory has its normal value. The operation result tells you whether a survey earned Full, Reduced, or Monitoring Only credit.

Monthly and Operations directives now share the next practical action: early research, preparing field weapons, restoring Elerium through a legally unlocked recipe, recovering samples or preparing for known human installations. A newly located base does not waive its assault research, trace sample or Warship requirement. Advice follows currently available prerequisite projects where possible. Confirmed intelligence distinguishes known bases and recovered human weapon upgrades from unconfirmed threats. Laser and rocket notices occur once after recovering foreign equipment; human research totals and hidden stocks do not reveal those upgrades in advance. A discovered base produces one tracing notice. None of these notices forces a battle or teleports responders.

Every operation shows the actual pending and delivered strategic cargo receipt in Operations and its result report. Scout returns intelligence; Abduct returns capture samples and modest resources; Harvest returns hull-appropriate resources, with dedicated Harvesters also collecting food and genetic stock; City Terror records its panic impact; Rural Terror yields modest intelligence. Native battle debrief separately recovers actual weapons, ammunition, bodies and field cargo. Stunned humans recovered from any operation contribute capture research proof, not only during Abduction. An abort retains only prisoners and cargo at the recognized extraction area or carried by returning units. A failed objective can still return an actually extracted prisoner without becoming a successful mission. The collecting craft must survive and return; merely completing a timer cannot teleport strategic cargo home, and reloading cannot duplicate its receipt. Research and fabrication requirements still apply to everything brought back.

If X-COM reaches a supported landing, the mission opens a playable battlemap. On Scout, Abduction, and Harvest responses, your aliens usually begin in their UFO and X-COM approaches from its transport. City Terror takes place at an urban site with civilians. Rural Terror uses rural terrain and a smaller objective.

If the UFO is brought down, recovery is resolved on the strategic map. Losing a hull can give X-COM recovered material, advance its research and strengthen future responses. A shootdown does not currently open a player-controlled crash-site battle. Do not continue an obviously compromised sortie solely to prove the crew can win a tactical fight. Getting the craft and its evidence home can be the larger victory.

After a concealed Scout finishes, read the report, wait for the real return and servicing, and inspect stores. Do not click Launch repeatedly because no battle occurred. Quiet reconnaissance is a legitimate result. Conversely, do not dismiss a response report as flavor text: the strategic model records enemy patrol analysis, ground-response observations and base traces through real operation outcomes and support chains.

RAID (Harvest)'S PURPOSE AND ITS RECEIPT: Harvest gives you a repeatable supply of resources and specimens. A quiet Scout Harvest can return two Earth Intelligence Reports and two Resource Caches; specialist craft can collect more. These are cargo, not instant additions to stores. Watch for Cargo pending in Operations, let the collecting craft return to its home base, then check Cargo delivered to base and its stores. Battle losses or an early retreat can reduce the return.

Resource Caches feed Reserve Processing, which combines a cache with Construction Materials and Allocation to make Elerium. The exact recipe remains in the fabrication appendix. Later Final Logistics combines a cache and a Harvest Yield with Allocation to supply Elerium, alloys, Construction Materials and food. Alien Food and Genetic Material support the existing organism fabrication recipes; their research requirements still apply. Intelligence Reports support the research sample chain. Harvest is therefore the resource job, while Rural Terror is the early deliberate fight. Quiet limited Scout/Surveyor Harvest also advances field sampling but does not award the full Harvest-chain victory contribution. Full Harvester/Warship strategic Harvest or a completed tactical Harvest objective advances that chain.

If X-COM interrupts Harvest, use the normal battle's actual recovery rules. Recover the physical Harvest Yield from the map and satisfy extraction. The report shows Harvest Yield recovered separately from queued strategic items. Native debrief recovers the actual sample; the abstract reward bridge does not duplicate it. An abort or crew wipe is not converted into automatic cargo. Destroying samples with explosives can leave you with a firefight victory but a failed extraction objective.

Battlemap controls and turns

The tactical battle is turn based. Select a friendly unit, inspect its time units, energy, health and morale, and move only as far as your planned action allows. Left-click selects or orders movement; right-click commonly changes facing or cancels a targeting action. Clicking a held weapon opens its action menu. Choose the shot, throw, prime or use action first, then the target. Check the menu's displayed TU cost instead of counting on a remembered value from a different weapon.

Native default keys include Tab for the next unit, I for Inventory, M for the minimap, K for kneeling, Home to center the selected unit, Page Up and Page Down to change the viewed level, Backspace for End Turn, and A for Abort. Key bindings can be changed in Options. The native Next Unit button also supports right-click for the previous unit. Hover over HUD icons for their tooltips; do not assume a numbered diagram found for another edition matches this interface.

Reserve buttons select None, Snap, Aimed or Auto TU reservation. They help keep enough TU for the chosen attack rather than letting movement spend everything. They do not force a reaction shot, and a weapon lacking that firing mode cannot conjure one. A unit needs a suitable weapon, ammunition, vision and the relevant reaction opportunity. Keep facing and line of sight in mind when positioning an overwatch unit.

At the start of a turn, locate visible threats before moving the whole squad. Check the level display, because an enemy above a doorway is not necessarily standing on the ground tile beneath it. Open the minimap for broad orientation, then return to the detailed map for firing lanes. Move one scout, update your information, and let supporting units act on that information. Sending every unit across the same exposed exit before seeing the opponent wastes the main advantage of a turn-based game.

TU is your action budget; energy is another constraint on sustained movement. A loaded, wounded or exhausted unit may not perform like its healthy counterpart. Encumbrance is particularly costly for light Sectoids. Carrying a rifle, launcher, grenades, smoke, flares, multiple clips and a body on one unit can leave it unable to do the job you selected it for. Divide burdens and keep a route back to the UFO.

Shots involve the operator's ability, weapon mode, circumstances, distance and actual obstruction. A displayed accuracy value does not remove walls from the trajectory or guarantee a hit. Aimed fire is useful when a safe unit can afford the time; snap fire preserves flexibility; automatic fire spends ammunition quickly and can threaten a broader area. Before shooting, examine what lies beyond the target. A miss through an ally's tile is still dangerous.

Kneeling can improve a shooting position and reduce exposure, but it costs time and is not hard cover. A kneeling unit in an open field can still die. Face the threatening direction, use terrain that actually blocks or interrupts enemy sight, and spread enough that one explosion does not remove your entire section. Avoid placing two operators directly behind the same doorway unless they can both survive the likely incoming attack.

Reaction fire rewards disciplined positioning. Reserve an attack, keep the shooter ready, and expose another unit only when support can see the enemy. Each enemy action can create a reaction opportunity according to the engine's combat rules; you cannot reserve TU after the enemy has already fired. When you must cross an observed lane, smoke, a different exit, a flank or suppressing pressure may reduce the risk more than another optimistic move.

Morale changes the plan. Casualties and frightening events can lead to panic or loss of control. Watch remaining morale and avoid turning one bad exchange into a chain of isolated deaths. Keep the section mutually supporting, recover endangered wounded units when practical, and accept an extraction when the mission's objective allows it. Bravery and command caste labels are not immunity to every tactical event.

Inventory changes during combat consume TU. Before reloading, check that the clip is compatible and accessible. Put routine spares where the carrier can retrieve them; do not wait until the final shot to discover they are on the ground at the starting tile. Many innate attackers have no conventional inventory at all. Their lack of a normal equipment screen is intentional, and their attack actions remain available through the native combat interface.

Night missions, stun, and extraction

Night landings restrict what you can observe. Do not equate darkness with complete safety: the enemy can still react to what it detects. Electro-Flares illuminate areas but can also reveal the route you are taking. Throw a flare to gain information about a likely contact area; do not surround every friendly tile with light before understanding the threat. Keep an unexposed shooter ready while a scout checks the newly visible ground.

Smoke is a tool for controlling sight and protecting movement, not a force field. It needs time to develop and can affect your own shooting and breathing. Use it at a dangerous UFO exit or withdrawal lane, then advance in measured steps. Avoid standing wounded units indefinitely in dense smoke. A doorway with smoke beyond it can conceal threats as well as friends; maintain a backup position inside the craft.

For Abduction, take the Small Launcher with Stun Bombs and enough ordinary weapons to protect the capture team. Stun damage can render a human unconscious without killing the specimen. Check blast placement and friendly spacing before firing. An unconscious human is a recoverable body, not a permanently secured prisoner the instant it falls. A stun victim can become difficult to recover if the surrounding lane stays under fire.

To extract during an abort, bring unconscious bodies into the recognized start or exit area, either on the floor there or carried by an eligible surviving alien there. The native debrief checks the body's actual position or carrier. It does not award every unconscious human on the map simply because you once saw it. For non-aborted victory, recovery is broader, but a living conscious alien still has to remain to secure the result. If every alien is dead or unconscious, cargo and survivors cannot rescue the mission score by themselves.

Harvest also requires actual cargo. Look for Harvest Yield in the tactical site, have a suitable operator secure it, and move it toward the UFO. On an abort, the carrier or floor position must satisfy recovery rules. Shooting all defenders without recovering yield can fail the Harvest objective. Likewise, carrying a sample while leaving no alien able to extract can turn an apparently finished objective into a failed operation.

Before choosing Abort, move everyone you intend to save into the extraction area and account for unconscious allies. The native debrief treats non-extracted player units as losses on an abort. A body carried back by a surviving alien can count; a wounded survivor hiding behind a tree outside the exit cannot. Read the abort screen and final debrief carefully. The fact that your objective was completed earlier in the battle does not waive extraction for a mission that requires it.

Do not use Abort as an automatic victory button. Scout prioritizes survival and return, but Abduction requires recovered captives, Harvest requires recovered materials, and Rural Terror requires enough X-COM casualties as well as a survivor. An orderly retreat can preserve your campaign even when it fails the current objective. That is a strategic choice, not proof of a scoring bug.

Battle plans by objective

Scout: gain contact information and preserve at least one extractable survivor. Start from the UFO's protection, establish a firing lane, and avoid unnecessary pursuit. If the Probe is involved, remember that its lone alien has no partner to cover reloads or body recovery. A light reconnaissance force should not copy the aggression of a full Warship section. A safe return can be sufficient for the objective, provided extraction rules are met.

Abduction: identify a human you can stun and retrieve, then build the fight around that retrieval route. Keep one lethal shooter covering the capture carrier. Use the Mind Probe for information when appropriate, but it is not a substitute for a stun weapon. The objective evaluator requires more than zero recovered captives. Killing every human can leave you with corpses and valuable gear but fail the intended Abduction purpose.

Harvest: locate the yield before chasing every enemy. A pair can secure cargo while another pair covers the approach. Keep enough TU for pickup, movement and protection. The evaluator requires recovered materials. When the exit is threatened, concentrate on a recoverable sample and surviving carrier rather than scattering the section across unrelated loot. Wilderness maps do not owe you civilian targets; this is biological/resource extraction rather than city terror.

Rural Terror: use Scout or Surveyor, engage the X-COM responders, and bring a survivor home. The casualty requirement is at least the site's minimum X-COM squad size, with a floor of one. Fleeing immediately does not count as a successful intimidation raid. Organize a controlled ambush around the UFO approach, with separate firing positions and a protected withdrawal lane. The operation offers modest strategic gains compared with full city pressure and is not a loophole for farming urban civilian effects.

City Terror: the urban map has civilians, buildings and many corners, but no tactical UFO interior. The objective evaluator requires at least eight civilians affected; the native city-terror debrief counts civilian deaths for that value. Stunned civilians are not a shortcut to this particular quota. Complete it before the last X-COM responder falls: neutralizing a combat force ends the battle. A terror body and a viable force must have been launched. Spread the squad, check upper floors and preserve enough force to withstand the X-COM response. The strategic site records a twenty-turn objective timer and a two-survivor planning value; those values are not a promise that simply waiting twenty turns or leaving two bodies on the map awards success.

Crash recovery: inspect which survivors and materials are still recoverable. At least some recovered material or an extracted alien can yield partial extraction. A downed UFO is already an enemy opportunity, so avoid doubling the loss by sacrificing the recovery team. Secure useful cargo when possible, deny exposure through a sensible exit, and accept that a partial result can still be better than a total wipe.

X-COM base assault: treat the facility layout as an interior assault with short lanes, dangerous corners and concentrated defenders. The evaluator accepts a destroyed base or damage of at least seventy-five. Native debrief damage can rise with X-COM casualties, but damaging a base is not automatically the same as neutralizing it for the campaign's final victory requirement. Use the debrief and strategic report to confirm the result. The base mission does not use ordinary UFO extraction.

Across all objectives, an unconscious opponent and a dead opponent can both contribute to the enemy casualty count, but only recoverable unconscious humans supply the appropriate captive result. Tactical casualties also drain the actual hidden X-COM manpower pool. Fight for the objective and recovery, then review the strategic consequences instead of judging every mission solely by the number of red units removed.

Midgame research and X-COM growth

Build the first tier around three connected branches: X-COM Response, Resource Survey and Capture Logistics. These produce Covert Pressure, which releases Surveyor and Carrier hulls. Surface Adaptation then depends on Covert Pressure, Human Autopsy and Abduction Protocol. Preserve a Human Corpse for the autopsy and make sure it reaches the researching base. The Human Autopsy reference page can be read before completing the research; reading a page is not research completion.

Open War Escalation follows Surface Adaptation and Skyranger Analysis, while High Command Release follows Open War Escalation and Radar Countermeasures. These are authorizations to use established alien assets under Earth's conditions, not a story in which Cydonia has just invented plasma or the species it already commands. The complete appendix lists every dependency and required sample so that a missing intermediate project can be traced systematically.

Keep research relevant to what your craft can actually return. If Beam Weapons analysis waits for a Laser Rifle, pursue useful atmosphere, patrol or logistics work instead of leaving Analysts idle indefinitely. Once the enemy fields lasers, recover a sample intact. If personal armor is absent, that analysis can wait; it does not authorize human armor manufacture anyway. Your first Warship is more urgent when a known X-COM base threatens the campaign than when you merely want a larger hangar occupant.

Human replication is a parallel equipment branch with thirty-eight recipes. It includes ballistic weapons and ammunition, heavy weapons and shell types, rockets, explosives, smoke, flares, lasers, medical/support tools, a Psi-Amp and aircraft armaments. It excludes human armor and human aircraft hulls. There is no Gauss family in this UFO Defense catalog. Some of these names have Xenopedia pages for reference, but a reference entry alone does not authorize buying or making the item.

Aircraft Replication needs Interceptor Pattern, Scout Flight and Recovered Human Aircraft Systems from a confirmed human aircraft loss. Ordinary human infantry loot does not produce those systems. Basic cannon and missile hardware then become available. Laser Cannon additionally needs Laser Replication and Heavy War Mandate; Plasma Beam and Fusion Ball hardware require Heavy War Mandate. Fit the actual armament and its ammunition to an eligible UFO weapon slot. Probe has no slot; your alien hull remains an alien hull after fitting human hardware.

Fabrication consumes inputs at the recipe's stated cost and work requirement. Manufacturing Terror Organism Gestation produces a reserve item; it is not automatically a new named Chryssalid Soldier appearing in Crew. Cybernetic Warframe Assembly likewise produces its reserve marker. Reinforcement purchase authorization and physical workshop outputs should be read separately. The full table lists each output exactly, including support documents and bundled ammunition.

X-COM research advances as its staffed bases survive. Exposure to your activity and captured UFO material can help the humans learn faster. Destroying a craft or leaving bodies behind can therefore hurt you long after the immediate loss.

The Beginner resistance receives a small forty-unit addition to its monthly emergency budget, preserving gradual paid expansion after the research slowdown. It still pays for each soldier, weapon, aircraft and new base.

Meanwhile X-COM operates a paid base and aircraft economy. Its staff, laboratories, workshops, stores, radar and craft readiness matter. Casualties, damaged bases and destroyed aircraft interrupt real capacity. Captured or recovered alien material helps its adaptation. An enemy that repeatedly retrieves downed UFOs can progress toward better weapons and the final counterattack even while you are completing familiar early missions.

The network can grow to eight active bases. Expansion is limited by campaign/difficulty priorities and a real reserve; a founding order spends 1,200 internal enemy budget units and takes 720 strategic hours before the new base becomes operational. A construction order does not supply an immediate radar, staff pool or free aircraft. The initial new-base packet is modest. Destroying the final active base cancels its pending overseas construction. Do not turn these source values into a promise that a specific numbered month always creates a specific base.

Use this growth to prioritize targets. A mature base with research and aircraft may be more dangerous than a newly founded outpost. Repeated field victories reduce manpower but can also raise alertness. Your goal is to convert tactical success into durable strategic advantage: recover proof, maintain Authority, disrupt infrastructure and preserve valuable craft. Cycling the same convenient early sortie forever may leave the most dangerous enemy base untouched.

Monthly reports and the late campaign

The Cydonian Monthly Report gives the High Order's judgment of your last month. Read the assessment, completed and failed operations, losses, recovered cargo, Authority change, X-COM threat, and the next directive. A good historical total will not excuse a month in which the invasion made no useful progress. The Operations screen has further mission and enemy details.

The ordinary assessment margin is alien strategic score minus X-COM threat score minus difficulty-scaled council pressure, adjusted for recent performance after the opening assessment. The adjustment is twelve per successful operation, minus eighteen per failed operation, twenty-four per UFO loss and two per personnel loss, plus a recovery bonus of twice captives plus half materials (capped at twenty-four), minus twenty-four for the assessment baseline. The overall adjustment is clamped between minus120 and plus80. This prevents a token monthly recon from making a capped late campaign permanently safe. It does not replace the doctrine or final-assault prerequisites. Exemplary requires a margin of at least eighty and readiness below fifty. Favorable begins at thirty-five; Satisfactory at minus ten; Unstable at minus forty. Unsatisfactory generally covers the next band down through minus eighty when readiness is below ninety; worse conditions are Critical. Doctrine-ready and human-final-assault cases have special precedence, and idle months after the opening assessment can override an otherwise comfortable margin.

Authority changes are +14 for Exemplary, +8 for Favorable, +2 for Satisfactory, -4 for Unstable, -9 for Unsatisfactory and normally -15 for Critical. A Critical final-defense trigger uses -12. Authority stays within zero through 220. Unstable, Unsatisfactory and Critical each extend the consecutive setback count; a better grade resets it. This gives you a meaningful recovery path, but an active enemy prevents indefinite stagnation. A Satisfactory, Favorable or Exemplary assessment can reduce stale assault readiness by six, twelve or twenty points toward the current real enemy pressure target. Bad grades grant no relief. A fully equipped X-COM force still uses its existing final-assault trigger; a lower old readiness peak does not cancel an already announced attack.

The first assessment allows preparation. After that, zero successful operations with active X-COM bases produces an idle assessment of Unsatisfactory or Critical. Building rooms and researching quietly for many months is therefore not a safe permanent strategy. Complete appropriate operations while the laboratories and workshops run. Do not accelerate months because the bank balance is comfortable and assume old reconnaissance totals will protect your standing forever.

For the late offensive, pursue X-COM Base Location and its prerequisites, obtain a real base trace, complete Base Assault, and buy a Warship after High Command Release. A base must be known to aliens and still active before it is a valid assault target. Click the revealed X-COM base, select ASSAULT, choose a ready Warship with the required crew and fuel, then launch. Other large hulls cannot replace the Warship merely because they can carry eight people.

Before assault, prepare portable ammunition, enough firepower for corridors, capture equipment if useful, and recovery support. Review the crew so that the entire opening specialist pool is not accidentally consumed by automatic assignment. Maintain another working craft for ongoing operations and emergency readiness. A base raid that damages infrastructure can weaken the enemy economy, but confirm neutralization in the result before treating it as the final campaign milestone. An X-COM base with no soldiers falls when your Warship reaches it; a defended base opens its normal battlemap.

At this stage, balance your campaign rather than chasing every research entry blindly. Intelligence, Harvest, Abduction, response exploitation and base suppression all matter. Some late projects extend supply and doctrine but do not directly set the victory flag. A large stockpile and Final Readiness research are helpful preparation; the exact ending rules are in the spoiler section. Keep a healthy, ready non-Probe craft available if the enemy's final-assault readiness is becoming alarming.

Ending conditions (spoilers)

Open complete ending spoilers and exact outcome rules

Stop here if you want to discover the final judgment through play. The rules below describe the actual campaign outcome checks, not just the names of late research projects.

Route A, strategic alien victory: the campaign must still be ongoing. The ledger needs at least eight Earth Intelligence Report contributions, two completed Harvest chains, two Abduction Sample contributions, two X-COM Response Report contributions and one neutralized X-COM base. Authority must be at least 150, Alien Intel at least 45, and Earth Infiltration score at least 120. Alien strategic score must also equal or exceed X-COM survival pressure. Meeting only the research tree, only the civilian quota, or only the Authority threshold is insufficient.

These are cumulative strategic checks. A physical sample used by a laboratory or workshop is not always identical to the ledger history. Read the actual monthly indicators and operation results rather than counting only icons in storage. A base damaged to the mission's minimum threshold is not guaranteed to satisfy the neutralized-base ledger. The strategic ending is checked before the human final-assault route in the ordinary update function.

When that victory triggers, Earth Capitulates. Humanity submits to Cydonian rule; the ending describes the collapse of resistance and a conquered, exploited world. The custom branch uses the original game's human-defeat ending assets for alien victory. That reversal is intentional. You are seeing the consequence for humanity, not an accidental statement that your alien campaign lost.

Route B, human attack followed by successful defense: X-COM must first be genuinely prepared. Global X-COM research must reach 100. At least one active base must have eight soldiers, positive scientists and laboratories, positive engineers and workshops, a Hyper-Wave Decoder, recovered UFO components, and a Cydonia assault stockpile of at least 100. That base also needs a ready, undamaged Skyranger with positive fuel and recovery capacity of at least eight. A readiness number alone does not bypass those infrastructure and craft requirements.

With those preparations, the assault triggers when readiness reaches 100, or when research is 100, global alertness at least ninety and X-COM survival pressure at least 160. The campaign changes to Defense of Cydonia and grants a 720-strategic-hour response window. This is a deadline, not an invitation to spend another year researching. It is measured by strategic hours rather than a guarantee about a particular displayed calendar month.

Choose a healthy, ready alien craft other than Probe with biological crew, no damage, no over-capacity load and no conflicting operation assignment. The Cydonia order popup opens when the final attack is announced. If you close it, reopen Operations and click DEFEND CYDONIA. In the order popup, choose DEFEND, cycle to the eligible craft and click LAUNCH. This action is on Operations, rather than the monthly assessment screen. The target UI still performs its crew and readiness validation. A mechanical platform alone cannot launch the defense, and a craft away on another mission cannot teleport its crew into it.

The battle is the native final-assault terrain used for an alien defense. Protect the Cydonian command objective and neutralize the human attackers. The debrief awards a held defense only when you did not abort, the objectives were not all destroyed, at least one alien remains conscious, human attackers existed, and those attackers are neutralized. Winning that defense gives alien victory even through this alternative final route. The ending again shows Earth's submission.

Route C, failed or abandoned final defense: aborting the Cydonia battle, losing the conscious alien force, failing the defense result, or allowing X-COM to destroy the command objectives yields alien defeat. If the 720-hour strategic response window reaches zero without the required defense, the campaign resolves as failed defense. The human-victory ending uses the original game's victory assets and the Cydonia Control Lost report. Retreat is not an extraction-based second chance in the final defense.

Route D, sustained strategic failure: four consecutive bad monthly assessments while at least one X-COM base remains active produce alien defeat. Here bad means Unstable, Unsatisfactory or Critical. A single disappointing month does not end the game by itself; successive idle or failing months can. The ending describes X-COM severing the Cydonian network. Keeping the clock running without missions is therefore an actual loss route, not merely inefficient play.

Route E, loss of the Earth-side network: if no player bases remain, the Geoscape records Alien Network Destroyed and resolves alien defeat. A large treasury or a high previous strategic score cannot operate an invasion with no surviving base network. Preserve functioning infrastructure as well as craft and troops.

Ordinary field losses also matter through casualties, lost UFOs, enemy recovery, Authority and threat escalation. They are steps toward strategic failure or the human counterattack, not a separate universal rule saying that any one lost Scout instantly ends the campaign. If a different native game-over condition or corrupted older save appears, inspect the actual message and retain the save for diagnosis rather than assuming it is one of these intended campaign routes.

Common questions

Why are there only five purchase rows at the start? That is the intended fresh catalog: Sectoid Soldier, Analyst, Fabricator, Probe and Scout. The purchase filter excludes inherited human stores and aircraft. As authorizations complete, the authored paid alien troop and hull catalog grows to twenty-one rows including the two staff types. Specialist caste rules with zero buy cost are not extra free purchase rows. Recovered and replicated human equipment comes through its own systems.

Why is there no Terror Craft? City terror requires Dreadnought or Warship. Scout and Surveyor can conduct the separate rural raid on valid ground, while Probe is Scout-only. Terror Deployment releases the Dreadnought and Cyberdisc purchases; High Command Release releases Warship. Check completed research, hangar space, transfer arrival, servicing, crew and a terror-body payload. Selecting a city does not grant the hull or body automatically.

Why does the popup say NEED CREW or NO ELIGIBLE CRAFT? First click the desired order. Check the selected craft family, READY status, remaining fuel, damage, assignment to another operation and capacity. Healthy biological reserves can be auto-assigned, but mechanical tanks cannot fill their slots. A crew already aboard another active craft is unavailable. Inspect Crew and Tanks separately, and use the popup's actual status message to narrow the problem.

Why is a research project missing? Complete every dependency and place the specified item at that base. Human Ballistics needs X-COM Structure, X-COM Response and a Rifle sample. Human Beam Weapons needs its analysis prerequisites and a Laser Rifle. Base Assault needs its late research chain and a real X-COM Base Trace. A similarly named manufactured model or a readable Xenopedia article is not automatically the missing project or sample.

Why is a visible recipe impossible to start? Visibility indicates authorization, not all production conditions. Check money, required items, workshop space, available Fabricators, current project reservations and requested quantity. A recipe may require physical Earth Intelligence, Resource Cache and response proof together. A base can own the research globally while lacking the local inputs. Reduce a batch to one to determine whether quantity is the immediate obstacle.

Can mechanical units carry my weapons? Cyberdiscs and Sectopods use Tanks and fixed native attacks. They do not use the ordinary carried-weapon inventory. Reapers, Chryssalids, Silacoids and Celatids are also innate attackers, but are biological and board through Crew. Portable-equipment species use Equipment. The separate categories preserve the units' roles; a missing inventory portrait is not a reason to assign a rifle to a Cyberdisc.

Why is Human Autopsy already readable? The reference article is intentionally available for viewing. Completing the research still requires Human, a Human Corpse, Analyst work and the project's normal gate. The corpse is consumed. Check Research completion instead of treating an opened report as an unlock flag.

Does OK - 5 SEC mean the report closes after five seconds? It indicates the selected Geoscape time speed when the report is dismissed. It is not an automatic report countdown. Read the text and click the acknowledgment control when ready, then inspect the clock before resuming.

Why did months of doing nothing lose the campaign? The High Order evaluates current activity, and the enemy economy keeps developing. After the opening assessment, a month with no successful operations and active X-COM bases receives an idle penalty. Four consecutive bad grades can end the invasion. Maintain a sensible mission loop while building and researching; use slower time and named saves when your next useful task is uncertain.

Why did a successful firefight fail Abduction or Harvest? Their objectives require recovered captives or materials, followed by required extraction. Dead humans are not live captives. Unconscious bodies abandoned outside the exit during an abort are not secured cargo. Harvest Yield left unsecured can fail materials recovery. Read the objective and the debrief, not just the casualty panel.

Why does rural terror have no civilians? The rural mission uses normal wilderness landing terrain and responder combat. It has a casualty-and-survivor objective. It does not import the city-terror quota. If you want the urban civilian mission, select a city and bring a Dreadnought or Warship with its required terror force.

Why can I read a human weapon page but not buy the weapon? Xenopedia contains references for replicated designs. Purchases remain filtered to authorized alien reinforcements and hulls. Recover the appropriate samples, research replication, fabricate the item, then load it. Armor analysis does not add human armor recipes, and Aircraft Replication does not create a Skyranger.

Why do equipment portraits or facility squares look different? Alien portraits leave room for the equipment slots. The purple base surface and dark grid lines show the facility cells. Their appearance does not change crew capacity, room size, or tactical armor.

Why is a marker difficult to see? Globe markers retain normal game behavior, including visibility at your current view and zoom. Rotate and zoom deliberately, use the relevant target or craft information, and check whether the flight has actually launched or returned. A purple interface does not replace the normal strategic map's visibility rules.

What should I keep for a useful bug report? Preserve a save before the problem, the current save, the exact visible message, the release version and a short sequence of actions. Include whether extra mods or an older save were used. For battle results, record the site type, objective, abort status, survivors, cargo and extraction positions. Those facts distinguish a rule misunderstanding from a reproducible defect far better than 'the mission did not work.'

Campaign checklist

Before research: identify the next project, complete every prerequisite, check its sample at the correct base, and assign Analysts within capacity. Keep useful backup projects available when a rare sample has not arrived. Before manufacture: check local inputs, cash, project space, staff and batch quantity. Preserve enough Elerium for flight and enough proof items for the next gate. Before purchase: check authorization, personnel or hangar space, cost and transfer delay.

Before launch: choose the right target type, select the order explicitly, choose a permitted ready hull, assign a sensible biological crew, load any required terror body, check fuel and damage, and verify portable weapons and compatible ammunition. Save. During battle: read the objective, identify the starting or exit area, establish mutual support, reserve meaningful TU, secure the actual sample or captive, and reassess when morale or losses change the plan.

Before abort: collect the people and bodies you intend to recover, place them in the recognized exit, and accept the objective consequences. After return: inspect the debrief, servicing, wounded units, stores, Authority and unlocked projects. Before a month boundary: ensure current operations have a purpose, review upkeep and readiness, and avoid spending all reserves on a building that will not yet help.

If your campaign feels stuck, locate the bottleneck rather than replacing the whole plan. Missing proof calls for a suitable operation. Missing local items calls for transfer or fabrication. Missing crew calls for recovery or reinforcements. Missing cash calls for a smaller queue and stronger Authority. Escalating X-COM infrastructure calls for patrol analysis, recovery denial and eventually base suppression. A final-defense warning calls for immediate preparation within its real response window.

Terror pressure and sustainable operations

Terror is a public pressure weapon. It does not replace food, genetic materials, construction supplies, specimen recovery or research. Pay for the craft and platforms, fuel each sortie, and preserve the crew. X-COM must still launch and travel using its real surviving bases and assets; there are no extra conjured defenders.

City and Rural Terror share a rolling thirty-day saturation window. Your first two successful attacks on fresh targets retain full strategic value. The next two yield half, and later successes a quarter. Repeating a target or a nearby landing zone compounds that reduction, down to a quarter. This measures recent successful attacks, not calendar months; saving and loading does not reset it. After thirty days a success stops contributing to saturation. Failed attacks and partial extraction do not consume a full success's place. This is diminishing value, not a launch quota.

The order screen previews full, 50% or 25% pressure. Final value depends on the successful raids already resolved when this operation finishes. Operations reports show the earned pressure standing. Saturation reduces strategic terror pressure, monthly standing and base progress/authority/intelligence bonuses. Actual impact records, recovered prisoners and equipment, native combat scores and X-COM casualties still count. Rural Terror remains the weaker form and never grants City Terror proof. Research and campaign victory still require the wider invasion objectives.

Use Harvest to sustain your resources, Abduction for specimens and intelligence, Recon to find and prepare fresh targets, and assaults to disrupt X-COM infrastructure. A larger paid base network provides reach and support; it does not erase saturation. Choose terror when its pressure or combat opportunity justifies the exposed sortie.

Campaign Pacing and Assault

Launch count is not a victory quota. To capitulate Earth, complete Final Readiness research, hold at least 75 pressure in three cities across two regions, resolve 36 tactical engagements with at least eight objectives completed, and neutralize an X-COM base or hold Cydonia. A complete X-COM base wipe reduces the battle requirement to 24. Research, useful returned samples, campaign standing, base intelligence and objectives determine progress. A calendar target is planning advice, not a timer that guarantees either ending.

A completed Rural Terror battle earns 18 monthly standing. Other completed tactical objectives earn 24. Fresh routine operations retain 12; repeated nearby unopposed reconnaissance still diminishes to 6 and then 0. Partial extraction grants 6; failed extraction grants no success standing. Casualties, failed operations and lost UFOs still reduce the monthly assessment. These values are assessment inputs, not cash payments or displayed battle scores.

Use your workshop: X-COM Response Analysis Cell converts an Earth Intelligence Report and Cydonian Allocation into a Response Report after the required study. It costs money and Fabricator time. The Abduction Field Kit supplies a Small Launcher, four Stun Bombs and a Mind Probe using paid native recipes and materials. Read each recipe before committing. Fabricated reports do not replace operation history, a real discovered base or the required research for a base assault.

Choose flight routes and targets deliberately. Repeated Scout operations within defended airspace can be intercepted. A battle victory does not cancel the cost of a different UFO being shot down. Use safer resource and abduction targets to recover, pursue available countermeasure research, and save for an appropriate heavy hull. Do not repeatedly replace lost Scouts and expect a good bank balance.

A researched Warship can assault a located X-COM base. If humans defend it, you fight through its base battlemap. Complete the objective to destroy the base. To retreat, bring survivors and recoverable items back to the entry lift; aborting does not count as a destroyed base.
